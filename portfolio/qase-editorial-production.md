# Turning Expert Recordings into Short-Form Content at Qase

**Jane Goleva · Freelance Editorial Producer · 2026**

The challenge in repurposing expert video is editorial, not just technical. You need to recognise which ideas have value on their own, understand who should hear them, and preserve their meaning when shortening the original.

At Qase, I worked with technical source material and a video editor to test an editorial production workflow.

## My approach

I identified the audience and potential stories in the original recordings, evaluated each story for clarity, relevance and source fidelity, and translated the editorial decisions into production briefs.

I then reviewed the edited videos for meaning, wording, structure and presentation. AI supported the analysis and review; editorial decisions remained human.

The technical experts provided the original arguments. I owned editorial selection, briefing and review. The video editor executed the cuts, graphics and rendering.

## Selected work

**Original video:** [Testing in the AI Era: Two Debts You Can't Ignore — Vitaly Sharovatov](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7yBMAVeBv6E)

Four short-form pieces developed from the original:

- [AI Can Create the Illusion of Quality](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/U8pQOs9yBtQ)
- [How Do We Preserve Understanding in AI Development?](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/QOFz_IG_Itc)
- [Why Prevention Matters More in the AI Era](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/qANL7_Avl40)
- [AI Made Coding Cheap, Not Understanding](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vAAFiNK_cD8)

## Result

These videos illustrate how one expert recording can support several distinct editorial angles. Each piece has its own argument and audience value, rather than simply being an excerpt chosen for its length.

The pilot established a repeatable selection, briefing and review process. It did not measure a causal improvement in audience reach or commercial performance.
